How to use Chord Changes

Chord Changes helps you find chord progressions, hear them, understand why they work and take them into your music app. You don’t need to know music theory to start; the theory is there when you want it.

Quick start

  1. Pick a feeling. In the Simple view, tap Uplifting, Melancholy, Dreamy or another feeling. A progression appears in the chart and starts playing.
  2. Optionally pick a style. Pop, Rock, Jazz, Lo-fi and the rest each have their own typical chords. Tap “Make another” as often as you like.
  3. Change anything you don’t like. Tap a chord to hear it, then try one of the swap ideas, or tap the small ▾ next to its name to pick a different chord.
  4. Take it with you. Press Export to download MIDI or notation, copy the chord chart, or print a lead sheet.
Simple and All toolsThe switch at the top right changes how much you see. Simple keeps to the essentials; All tools shows everything described below. Your choice is remembered.

Finding your way around

On a computer the screen has five parts, with your chord chart in the middle:

Top barLibrary (save and open), Help, and the Simple / All tools switch

On a smaller laptop or tablet, Create slides out from the left when you press Create. On a phone, a tab bar under the title switches between Create, Chart, Chord and Tools, one screen at a time.

Making a progression

Open Create → Generate.

  • Mood sets the feeling: Uplifting, Melancholy, Dreamy, Dark, Tense, Nostalgic, Epic or Mysterious. Pick one, or none.
  • Genre sets the style: Pop, Rock, Blues, Jazz, R&B / Neo-soul, Gospel, Funk, Country, Folk, EDM, Metal, Bossa nova, Lo-fi and Film score. Pick several to blend them.
  • What this genre plays shows each picked genre’s typical chords in your key; tap one to hear it. Chords shown faded are outside Chords allowed. Turn on Let the genre choose its chords to use them anyway.
  • Predictable ↔ Surprising controls how often a chord from outside the key sneaks in.
  • Length and Chord changes set how many bars and how often the chord changes. Keep my mode stops Generate from switching to another mode.
  • Press Generate. The box above the button explains in plain words what Generate is about to do.

Keeping the chords you like

Select a chord and press Lock in the Chord panel (a small padlock appears on it in the chart). Regenerate unlocked then refills only the other chords and keeps every chord’s length. Empty slots (shown as ?) are always refilled.

Key, meter and limits

Create → Key and limits holds the key, mode, time signature and tempo, and Chords allowed: the most colourful chord types you want anywhere in the app. The presets Simple, Color, Jazz and Everything are starting points; tap the families (Power, Sus, Add & 6, 7ths, 9 · 11 · 13, Altered, Dim & aug, Slash) to fine-tune. Plain major and minor chords are always allowed.

Writing a chart as text

Open Edit as text under the chart to type or paste a progression, then press Load (or ⌘/Ctrl + Enter). Every change you make in the chart is written back here too.

[Verse]
C | Am | F | G:2 G7:2
[Chorus]
F | G | Em | ?
  • One line per phrase; | between bars. Chords sharing a bar split it evenly.
  • [Verse] or Chorus: at the start of a line labels a section.
  • :2 after a chord gives it an exact number of beats.
  • ? is an empty slot you want ideas for; % repeats the previous chord; an empty bar | | holds the chord for another bar.
  • Chord names are read flexibly: Cmaj7, CΔ7, C-7, Cø, F#m7b5, Bbsus4, Gmaj7(9), D/F# and many more.

Swap ideas

Select a chord and open the Swap ideas tab. Ideas are grouped (chords that do the same job, recolourings, borrowed chords, chords that lead into the next one, and more). Each idea shows:

  • its theory name, such as “Secondary dominant (V7/vi)” or “Modal interchange”;
  • a sentence about this exact swap: which notes it keeps, whether it sounds brighter or darker, and where it goes next;
  • a voice-leading line: which notes are held and how far each voice moves from the chord before;
  • ▶ to hear it between its neighbours, A/B to loop the original and the idea bar by bar, and Put in this slot to use it.

Keep note filters the ideas to those containing a note you choose, for example your melody note.

Playing and hearing

  • Play in the bottom bar plays the chart; Loop repeats it. Space also starts and stops playback.
  • Sound: Piano or Guitar. Guitar plays the actual guitar shapes in your tuning and capo.
  • Style: piano can play block chords, a ballad (bass, then chords), a pop pulse or an arpeggio; guitar can strum once, play a strum pattern (down, down-up, up-down-up) or fingerpick.
  • Smooth voice leading (in Chart tools) keeps the piano’s notes close from chord to chord; Fixed root position plays every chord the plain way.
  • Click adds a metronome.

Guitar and piano shapes

The Chord tab shows the chord’s notes, guitar shapes and piano voicings. The shape marked Smoothest path is the one playback uses, chosen so your hand moves as little as possible. Tap any shape or voicing to hear it, then ▶ In context to hear it between its neighbours or Use for this chord to make playback use it.

Pick a Tuning (standard, half or whole step down, Drop D, Drop C, double drop D, DADGAD, open G, D, E or C) and a Capo. Find the easiest tuning for this chart compares every tuning and capo position for your chords and recommends one, keeping standard tuning unless another is clearly easier.

The Library at the top right saves progressions by name in this browser. You can open, rename, duplicate and delete them (with undo), export the whole library as a file for backup, import it on another computer, and paste a share link or code to open it.

More tools

  • Chord finder (under the chart): place fingers on a fretboard or press piano keys, and it names the chord and shows it on the other instrument.
  • Harmonize a melody: type one note per beat (for example E D C D | E E E -, with - to hold and . for a rest) and get three to five progressions, ranked by how many strong-beat melody notes are chord tones.
  • Keys and modes: the chords in your key, and how well your chart fits each mode on the same tonic.
  • Tension curve above the chart shows how much each chord pulls away from home.
  • Show voice leading draws each voice’s path through the chart, marking held notes and big leaps.
  • Quiz me hides the colours and labels and asks what each chord does; your score is kept.

Rhythmic cycles and drones

The time signature menu also offers Carnatic and Hindustani cycles (Adi talam, Rupaka, Misra and Khanda chapu, Teental, Jhaptal, Rupak taal, Ektal) as grids for placing chord changes, and Create offers a Sa–Pa drone under a mode. These are sketching aids, not authoritative accounts of those traditions.

If you hear nothing

  • Phones and browsers only allow sound after you tap the page, so the first tap on Play is what starts it.
  • Check the volume and where the sound is going (Bluetooth, headphones).
  • Open Help → Sound check. It plays two test beeps by different routes and shows what your device reports, which narrows down where the sound stops.

Your data

Everything stays in your browser: your current chart, library, settings and quiz score are saved in this browser’s local storage on this device. There are no accounts and no tracking. Clearing your browser data clears them, so export the library now and then if it matters to you.
